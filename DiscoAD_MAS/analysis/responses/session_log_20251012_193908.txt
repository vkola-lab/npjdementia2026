### 📐 **Step 2: Primary Statistical Analysis – Quantifying the Association Using Survival Models**

**Objective:** Test whether higher educational attainment is associated with reduced risk of dementia over time, accounting for confounding factors.

**Analytical Approach:**  
- Use **Cox proportional hazards regression** as the primary model due to its suitability for time-to-event data (dementia onset).  
- Model structure:  
  ```plaintext
  hazard(t | education) = hazard₀(t) × exp(β₁·education + β₂·covariates)
  ```
- Include **key covariates** such as:  
  - Age at baseline  
  - Gender  
  - Income/SES  
  - Physical activity  
  - Cardiovascular comorbidity history  
  - Genetic markers (if available)  
- Apply **time-varying covariates** if follow-up periods allow tracking changes in lifestyle or health.  

**Robustness Checks:**  
- Stratify analysis by age group (e.g., <60, 60–70, >70) to examine heterogeneity.  
- Test proportionality assumption via Schoenfeld residuals; if violated, use time-dependent Cox models or restricted cubic splines.  

**Effect Size & Interpretation:**  
- Report **hazard ratios (HRs)** with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).  
- Calculate **adjusted HR per additional year of education**, e.g., “each additional year of education is associated with a 5% lower hazard of dementia.”  
- Perform **power analysis** pre-post estimation using simulation (via `pwr` or `simr`) to confirm sufficient sample size to detect moderate effects (e.g., HR = 0.85, α=0.05, power ≥80%).  

> ⚠️ Avoid linear regression for time-to-event data—this would incorrectly treat dementia as a binary outcome without accounting for time-to-onset.

### 🤖 **Step 3: Advanced Analytical Approaches – Uncovering Complex Patterns and Mechanisms**

**Objective:** Go beyond simple association to uncover potential mechanisms, interactions, and nonlinear dynamics.

**Approaches:**  
1. **Mediation Analysis (Cognitive Reserve Pathway):**  
   - Test whether education reduces dementia risk through cognitive reserve (e.g., measured by processing speed, memory performance, brain volume).  
   - Use **bootstrapped mediation models** (e.g., Baron & Kenny, or Hayes’ PROCESS macro) to estimate indirect effects.  
   - Example: Education → Cognitive reserve → Lower dementia risk.  

2. **Moderation Analysis:**  
   - Examine whether the protective effect of education varies by genetics (e.g., APOE ε4 carrier status), socioeconomic status, or baseline cognition.  
   - Fit interaction terms (education × moderator) in the Cox model and test significance (p < 0.05).  

3. **Machine Learning Pattern Detection (Optional but valuable):**  
   - Train a **random forest classifier** or **gradient boosting model** to predict dementia onset from baseline features.  
   - Extract feature importance rankings: does education consistently rank high?  
   - Compare predictive accuracy across models (AUC, Brier score) to validate clinical relevance.  
   - Use SHAP values to interpret individual predictions and visualize how education contributes to risk prediction.  

> 💡 This step allows detecting subtle or non-linear relationships missed by traditional regression (e.g., thresholds or diminishing returns).

### 🔁 **Step 4: Validation and Robustness Assessment – Ensuring Scientific Generalizability**

**Objective:** Confirm findings under varied conditions and assumptions to prevent spurious conclusions.

**Strategies:**  
- **Cross-validation (k-fold or time-split):**  
  - Split data into training/validation sets (e.g., 70%/30%) to evaluate model generalizability.  
  - For survival models: use time-dependent splits (e.g., early vs. late follow-up).  
- **Sensitivity Analyses:**  
  - Exclude extreme outliers (after outlier identification in Step 1).  
  - Re-analyze using different definitions of "education" (e.g., highest degree vs. total years completed).  
  - Adjust for selection bias using inverse probability weighting (IPW) if enrollment was non-random.  
- **Alternative Modeling Strategies:**  
  - Compare results using **logistic regression** (for binary dementia at endpoint) and **survival models** to assess consistency.  
  - Consider **accelerated failure time (AFT) models** if the underlying process appears to accelerate with education.  
- **Confidence Intervals & Significance Testing:**  
  - Ensure all estimates report 95% CIs.  
  - Flag results with wide CIs (>0.2) as uncertain.  
  - Report p-values cautiously—emphasize effect size and biological plausibility over significance alone.  

> 🛑 Avoid overfitting: Never apply complex ML models without cross-validation or external validation.
