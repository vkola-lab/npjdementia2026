## 📐 Step 2: Primary Statistical Modeling – Multivariate Survival Analysis  
*(Goal: Directly test the association between age and dementia incidence, adjusting for known risk factors)*

**Why survival analysis?**  
Dementia incidence is **time-to-event**, often with long follow-up periods and censored observations (subjects not yet developing dementia). Standard logistic regression assumes fixed time points but misses temporal dynamics.

**Approach: Cox Proportional Hazards Model**  
- Dependent variable: Time until first dementia event (defined by `Incident dementia during UDS follow-up_*` flags), with censoring defined as no dementia development by end of follow-up.
- Covariates:  
  - Age (`Subjects_age_at_visit`) as continuous predictor  
  - Key confounders:  
    - Education (`Years_of_education`)  
    - Sex (`Subject's sex_Male`)  
    - Race (as categorical: White, Black, Asian, etc.)  
    - Hypertension, diabetes, stroke/VBI history, B12 deficiency  
- Interaction terms: Consider interaction between age and education, or age × hypertension to assess **heterogeneity** in effect.

**Model evaluation:**  
- Check proportional hazards assumption via Schoenfeld residuals and log-log plots.  
- Report hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and p-values.  
- Perform goodness-of-fit testing (e.g., deviance statistic).

🟢 *Alternative if HR assumptions fail:* Switch to parametric survival models (Weibull/Gompertz) or semi-parametric accelerated failure time (AFT) models to compare results.

✅ *Scientist Flexibility:* Use `survival` package in R or `lifelines` in Python. Apply bootstrapping to estimate bias in HR estimates.

# Since age is continuous, we'll create age groups (e.g., quartiles) for categorical analysis
def create_age_groups(data, bins=4):
    """Create age groups using equal-width binning."""
    bins = np.quantile(data, np.linspace(0, 1, bins + 1))
    labels = [f'{int(bins[i]):d}-{int(bins[i+1]):d}' for i in range(len(bins)-1)]
    return pd.cut(data, bins=bins, labels=labels, include_lowest=True)

chi2_stat, p_value, dof, expected = stats.chi2_contingency(contingency_table)
