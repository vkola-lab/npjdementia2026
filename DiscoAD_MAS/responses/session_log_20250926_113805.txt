To rigorously assess whether current aspirin use is statistically and clinically linked to reduced Stroop interference (a marker of executive function/cognitive control).

---

### Option 1: Linear Regression with Adjustment for Confounders  
- Use *total Stroop interference score* as the dependent variable.  
- Include *reported current use of an anticoagulant or antiplatelet agent* (as a binary indicator) as the primary predictor.  
- Adjust for key confounders: age, number of prior strokes, MMSE score, year of last stroke.  
- Interpret effect size (e.g., β coefficient) and p-values to determine significance.  
- Strengths: Clear causal inference under controlled conditions; standard in epidemiology.  
- Caveat: Assumes linearity and normality; may miss non-linear relationships.

---

### Option 2: Stratified Analysis by Stroke History  
- Split participants into groups based on *More than one stroke reported*: yes/no.  
- Within each group, perform separate regressions or t-tests comparing Stroop interference between aspirin users and non-users.  
- Explore interaction effects: Does aspirin benefit only those with fewer strokes?  
- Strengths: Reveals heterogeneity in treatment effects; identifies subgroups where aspirin may be more effective.  
- Caveat: May reduce power per stratum if small sample sizes exist.

---

### Option 3: Propensity Score Matching (PSM)  
- Build a propensity score model predicting aspirin use based on age, stroke history, MMSE, time since stroke.  
- Match aspirin users with non-users using nearest neighbor matching (1:1 or 1:k).  
- Compare Stroop interference scores between matched pairs.  
- Strengthens internal validity by reducing selection bias.  
- Strengths: Addresses unmeasured confounding; mimics randomization.  
- Caveat: Requires careful modeling assumptions; sensitive to omitted variables.

---

### Option 4: Mediation Analysis  
- Test whether *cognitive decline (via MMSE)* mediates the relationship between aspirin use and Stroop interference.  
- Model: Aspirin → MMSE → Stroop interference.  
- Assess indirect effect (mediation path) and total effect.  
- Strengths: Explores biological plausibility; shows if aspirin improves cognition through known pathways.  
- Caveat: Requires strong theoretical grounding; assumes linear mediation.

---

### Option 5: Non-parametric Alternatives & Robustness Checks  
- Replace regression with Mann–Whitney U test (for two-group comparisons) or Kruskal–Wallis test (for >2 groups).  
- Apply bootstrapping to estimate confidence intervals without assuming normality.  
- Test sensitivity to outliers using robust regression techniques (e.g., Huber loss).  
- Strengths: Resilient to violations of distributional assumptions; enhances reliability.  

---

### Option 6: Interaction Modeling (Age × Aspirin)  
- Add interaction terms such as `age × aspirin` into regression models.  
- Investigate whether older patients show weaker or stronger responses to aspirin.  
- Visualize interaction plots alongside analysis.  
- Strengths: Identifies age-related nuances; supports personalized medicine insights.
