### ✅ **Research Strategy: Testing the Association Between Age and Dementia Incidence**

> *Objective*: To determine whether higher age at visit is significantly and independently associated with an increased likelihood of meeting dementia criteria (Met_criteria_for_dementia = 1), while accounting for potential confounding factors and assessing effect magnitude across subgroups.

---

## 🔍 Step 1: Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) – Establish Baseline Patterns & Quality

**Goal**: Understand distributional properties, identify outliers, and assess preliminary associations between age and dementia status.

**Actions**:

1. **Distribution Analysis**:
   - Plot histograms/density plots of `Subjects_age_at_visit` and `Met_criteria_for_dementia`.
   - Perform Shapiro-Wilk or Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests for normality of age distributions within dementia cases vs. non-cases.
   - Check skewness/kurtosis; consider log-transformation if necessary due to right-skew.

2. **Descriptive Statistics by Dementia Status**:
   - Compute mean, median, standard deviation, IQR of age for those with (`Met_criteria_for_dementia == 1`) and without dementia (`== 0`).
   - Report prevalence of dementia by age group (e.g., deciles or 5-year bins).

3. **Outlier Detection**:
   - Use IQR method or Z-scores (>3 SD) to flag extreme ages (>90 years or <30 years).
   - Investigate and document reasons for outliers (e.g., data entry error, misclassification); exclude only if justified via domain knowledge.

4. **Visualization**:
   - Create a **boxplot** of age stratified by dementia status.
   - Overlay a **density plot** showing overlap between age distributions of dementia and non-dementia groups.
   - Generate a **scatter plot** of age vs. CDR_sum_of_boxes (to explore severity trends).

> 💡 *Why?* This step establishes biological plausibility and identifies potential deviations from expected patterns (e.g., early-onset dementia clusters). It also flags data issues before formal modeling.

---

## 📊 Step 2: Primary Statistical Test – Logistic Regression with Adjustment for Confounders

**Goal**: Quantify the association between age and dementia incidence using adjusted odds ratios (ORs), controlling for known risk factors.

**Model**:
```python
logit(P(dementia)) = β₀ + β₁(age) + β₂(sex) + β₃(education) + β₄(HispanicLatino_ethnicity) + β₅(race indicators) + ε
```

**Key Components**:

- **Dependent variable**: `Met_criteria_for_dementia` (binary: 0 or 1)
- **Primary predictor**: `Subjects_age_at_visit` (continuous)
- **Covariates**:
  - `Subjects_sex`
  - `Years_of_education`
  - `HispanicLatino_ethnicity`
  - `Race_is_2.0`, `Race_is_5.0` (as binary indicators)

✅ **Justification**:
- Logistic regression is ideal for predicting probability of a dichotomous outcome like dementia diagnosis.
- Adjusting for sex, education, and ethnicity controls for established confounders (e.g., women may have different dementia risks, lower education linked to higher risk).
- Provides **odds ratio (OR)** per year of age increase, along with **95% confidence intervals (CI)**.

**Effect Size & Interpretation**:
- Report OR per one-year increment in age (e.g., "For every additional year of age, the odds of dementia increase by X%, after adjusting for covariates").
- Include **p-values**, **AIC/BIC** for model fit comparison.

> ⚠️ *Note*: Avoid treating age as a categorical variable unless exploring nonlinearities (see Step 3). Keep it continuous initially for simplicity and interpretability.

---

## 🔁 Step 3: Advanced Modeling – Survival Analysis (Time-to-Dementia Incidence)

**Goal**: Address temporal dynamics — dementia is not just a cross-sectional presence but a disease progression over time.

**Approach**: Cox Proportional Hazards Model

**Model**:
```python
h(t | age, covariates) = h₀(t) × exp(β₁·age + β₂·sex + ... )
```

**Variables**:
- Time-to-event: Defined as time from first visit to dementia onset (or censoring at last follow-up).
- Event: `Met_criteria_for_dementia == 1` (only when diagnosed post-visit).

**Critical Actions**:
1. **Define event times**:
   - Use `Form_date_year` to calculate time since baseline (assume first visit = reference point).
   - Assign follow-up duration until next visit or end of study.
   - Censor observations where no dementia was observed during follow-up.

2. **Fit Cox model**:
   - Include age as continuous predictor.
   - Add interactions (optional): e.g., age × sex to check differential effects.
   - Assess proportional hazards assumption using Schoenfeld residuals or scaled Schoenfeld plots.

3. **Output**:
   - Hazard ratio (HR) per year of age.
   - 95% CI and p-value.
   - Time-dependent hazard curves (visualize how risk evolves with age).

> 💡 *Advantage*: More accurate than cross-sectional models because it accounts for timing of diagnosis and longitudinal development.  
> ✅ Particularly powerful when considering that dementia develops gradually and is more likely in older individuals over time.

## 🧪 Step 4: Robustness Checks & Validation

**Goal**: Validate findings under varying assumptions and conditions to ensure generalizability.

**Sub-analyses**:

| Analysis | Purpose |
|--------|--------|
| **Stratified analysis by sex/race** | Check if age effect differs across subgroups (e.g., men vs. women, racial minorities). Use interaction terms or separate models. |
| **Non-linear age effects** | Fit restricted cubic splines (using `splines` or `polynomials`) to test curvature in age-dementia relationship. Example: Is the risk accelerating beyond midlife? |
| **Machine Learning (ML) Baseline** | Train a random forest classifier using `age`, `education`, `sex`, etc., to predict dementia. Compare out-of-sample performance and feature importance. Evaluate whether age ranks among top predictors. |
| **Sensitivity analysis** | Remove outliers (if identified), exclude younger adults (<60 years), or limit to elderly (>70 years) to see stability of results. |
| **Cross-validation** | Apply k-fold CV (e.g., 5-fold) on the logistic/survival model to estimate predictive accuracy and avoid overfitting. |

**Validation Metrics**:
- Area Under Curve (AUC) for classification models.
- Concordance index (c-index) for survival models.
- Bootstrap-based confidence intervals (with 1000 resamples).

> 🔍 *This ensures that the primary finding is not driven by noise or specific subsets of the data.*

# Compute 95% confidence interval for the age coefficient using bootstrapping
def bootstrap_coefficient(X, y, n_bootstraps=1000):
    boot_coef = []
    for _ in range(n_bootstraps):
        idx = np.random.choice(len(X), size=len(X), replace=True)
        X_boot = X[idx].reshape(-1, 1)
        y_boot = y[idx]
        model_boot = LogisticRegression(solver='liblinear', penalty='l1', C=1.0, random_state=42)
        model_boot.fit(X_boot, y_boot)
        boot_coef.append(model_boot.coef_[0][0])
    return np.percentile(boot_coef, [2.5, 97.5])

boot_ci = bootstrap_coefficient(X, y)
