### ✅ Step 3: Survival Analysis Using Cox Proportional Hazards Model  
*(Objective: Evaluate time-to-dementia incidence — capturing latency and dynamic risk over time)*

**Model Specification:**  
```plaintext
h(t | X) = h₀(t) ⋅ exp(β₁⋅age + β₂⋅sex + β₃⋅education + ...)
```
Where `h(t)` is the hazard of developing dementia at time `t`.

**Key Advantages Over Logistic Regression:**  
- Accounts for *time-dependent exposure*: age increases continuously throughout life  
- Can assess cumulative risk and provide hazard ratios (HRs) instead of ORs  
- Allows for modeling of delayed onset — crucial since dementia typically emerges after midlife  

**Analysis Steps:**  
- Define time variable implicitly: assume follow-up duration based on visit timing (if not available, treat age as continuous predictor in a static model).  
- Fit Cox model with age as primary predictor, adjusting for sex, education, race, and ethnicity.  
- Conduct proportional hazards check using scaled Schoenfeld residuals. If violated, apply time-varying covariates or stratify.  
- Report HRs with 95% CIs; interpret clinically (e.g., HR > 1.1 per decade suggests increasing risk).

🔹 *Why?* Superior to cross-sectional or binary models when studying progression of disease. Especially powerful given that dementia develops gradually.

> ⚠️ Important: If longitudinal follow-up data exist beyond current dataset (e.g., repeated visits), this could become a full cohort analysis. Since we lack such data now, we treat age as a baseline predictor with time-as-a-variable assumption.
