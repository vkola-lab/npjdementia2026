## ⚖️ Step 2: Primary Statistical Test – Cox Proportional Hazards Model (Time-to-Dementia Incidence)

**Goal:** Evaluate the independent association between age and dementia incidence, adjusting for key confounders.

**Why this model?**  
Age is a time-varying exposure and dementia is a time-to-event outcome. The **Cox PH model** is ideal because:
- It handles censored observations naturally (e.g., subjects still cognitively intact at study end).
- It allows adjustment for covariates such as hypertension, diabetes, education, sex, race, and prior cardiovascular events.
- It provides hazard ratios (HRs) with confidence intervals (CIs), enabling interpretation of relative risk increase with age.

**Model specification:**
```plaintext
Cox regression:
h(t | X) = h₀(t) × exp(β₁·age + β₂·hypertension + β₃·diabetes + ... + βₙ·race + βₘ·sex)
```

**Variables included:**
- **Predictor**: `Subjects_age_at_visit` (continuous)
- **Outcome**: Time-to-dementia event (defined via `Met criteria for dementia_Yes` or `Cognitive status at UDS visit_Dementia`)
- **Time variable**: Duration from baseline to dementia diagnosis or censoring (use `Day_permanently_moved_to_nursing_home` as proxy for transition to care — but validate against actual dementia diagnosis dates if available; otherwise use follow-up duration as surrogate).

**Key outputs to report:**
- Hazard ratio (HR) for each additional year of age.
- 95% CI for HR.
- p-value (test for significance).
- Model fit (log-likelihood, AIC/BIC, Schoenfeld residuals for proportional hazards assumption).

**Robustness checks:**
- Run the same model with **age grouped into quartiles** to assess non-linear associations.
- Include interaction terms (e.g., age × sex, age × diabetes) to detect differential effects across subgroups.

> ✅ *Outcome of this step*: Quantitative estimate of how age increases dementia risk, adjusted for clinical and demographic confounders.
