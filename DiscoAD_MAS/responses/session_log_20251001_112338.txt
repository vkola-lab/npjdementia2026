#### ✅ Statistical Tests & Assumptions:
| Test | Purpose | Assumptions |
|------|--------|-----------|
| Descriptive Summary Statistics | Central tendency, dispersion, skewness, kurtosis | None; standard descriptive analytics |
| Shapiro-Wilk / Kolmogorov–Smirnov | Normality of residuals and key predictors | Must be met for parametric inference |
| Levene’s Test | Homogeneity of variance across groups | Critical for ANOVA/regression |
| Pearson correlation | Initial bivariate association check | Linear, multivariate normality |

> Justification: This ensures sufficient power even under conservative effect sizes.

#### 🔍 Validation Strategies:
- **Cross-validation**: 10-fold cross-validation on training set (if applicable); use bootstrapped confidence intervals (BCIs).
- **Residual diagnostics**:
  - QQ-plots, scale-location plots, residual vs fitted plots
  - Check for heteroskedasticity, outliers, multicollinearity (VIF < 5)
- **Model fit**: R² adjustment, adjusted R² > 0.30 considered meaningful
- **Effect size reporting**: Cohen’s f² (for regression models), η²

#### ⚠️ Limitations & Mitigation:
- **Assumption sensitivity**: If normality fails, switch to non-parametric alternatives (see Plan 2).
- **Multicollinearity risk**: High correlation between age and APOE may inflate variance inflation. Consider centering or principal components.
- **Unmeasured confounders**: Conduct sensitivity analysis excluding participants with depression, hypertension, or stroke history.

#### 💡 Interpretation Guidelines:
- A p-value < 0.05 indicates statistical significance.
- Report effect size (e.g., “A one-unit increase in tau PET burden is associated with a −0.32 trial decrease in backward digit span, β = –0.32, 95% CI [–0.41, –0.23], p < 0.001”).
- Emphasize **independence from age and APOE genotype** via inclusion/exclusion subanalyses.

> ✅ Strengths: Transparent, interpretable, replicable, meets traditional journal standards.

#### ✅ Statistical Tests & Assumptions:
| Method | Purpose | Assumptions |
|-------|--------|------------|
| Spearman rank-order correlation | Bivariate association | No requirement for linearity or normality |
| Mann-Whitney U test (group comparisons) | Compare digit span by high vs low tau quartiles | Only requires ordinal or continuous data |
| Kruskal-Wallis H test | Compare groups across tau quartiles | Non-parametric alternative to ANOVA |
| Robust regression (Theil-Sen or Huber M-regression) | Regression modeling | Resistant to outliers, does not assume normality |

> Avoid OLS regression unless normality and homoscedasticity are verified via visual inspection.

#### 📏 Sample Size & Power Analysis:
- Use **non-parametric power simulations** (via R packages like `pwr`, `simr`) to estimate required n.
- With moderate effect size (Spearman r ≈ 0.30), require **n ≥ 75** for 80% power (estimated).

> Bootstrapping can validate power across different subsamples.

#### 🔍 Validation Strategies:
- **Bootstrap resampling (with replacement)**:
  - Generate 1,000 bootstrap samples to compute bias-corrected confidence intervals (BCa).
  - Assess stability of median effects across iterations.
- **Outlier detection**:
  - Use Cook’s distance (in robust regressions) or Mahalanobis distance.
- **Robustness checks**:
  - Reanalyze after removing extreme values (< 1st or > 99th percentile).
- Performance metric: **Sensitivity of rank-based associations** to removal of top/bottom 5%.

#### ⚠️ Limitations & Mitigation:
- Loss of interpretability compared to OLS (less intuitive coefficients).
- May miss nonlinear relationships (consider adding polynomial terms in robust splines later).
- Cannot easily extract standardized beta weights.

#### 💡 Interpretation Guidelines:
- "Higher tau PET burden correlates weakly but significantly with poorer digit span backward performance (Spearman ρ = 0.32, 95% BCa CI [0.18, 0.45], p < 0.001)."

> ✅ Strengths: Tolerant of real-world data irregularities; ideal for preliminary exploratory work.
