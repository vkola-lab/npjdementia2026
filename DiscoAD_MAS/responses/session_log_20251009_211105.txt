#### ⚙️ Step 2: Primary Statistical Test – Multivariable Logistic Regression with Survival Adjustment

**Objective**: Quantify the association between age and dementia incidence, adjusting for confounders and controlling for temporal dynamics.

**Analytical Plan**:

1. **Define exposure and outcome**:  
   - Exposure: `Subjects_age_at_visit` (continuous)  
   - Outcome: `Met_criteria_for_dementia` (binary)

2. **Model specification**:  
   Fit a **multivariable logistic regression model**:
   $$
   \log\left(\frac{p}{1-p}\right) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 (\text{age}) + \beta_2 (\text{sex}) + \beta_3 (\text{education}) + \beta_4 (\text{race}) + \beta_5 (\text{MMSE}) + \beta_6 (\text{CDR})
   $$  
   Include covariates known to influence dementia risk (sex, education, race, cognition scores).  

   > ✅ Why? Adjusts for known confounders and provides odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) per year increase in age.

3. **Assessment of linearity and interaction**:  
   - Check if the relationship between age and dementia is linear using polynomial terms (quadratic term: age²) or splines.  
   - Test for interaction effects (e.g., age × sex or age × race) to explore heterogeneity.

4. **Effect size reporting**:  
   Report **adjusted ORs with 95% CIs** and interpret clinically meaningful thresholds (e.g., OR > 1.1 per decade of age).

> 💡 *Alternative if linearity fails*: Replace with **fractional polynomial regression** or **restricted cubic spline models** to capture nonlinear trends.

#### 🕰️ Step 3: Survival Analysis – Time-to-Dementia Modeling

**Objective**: Strengthen causal interpretation by accounting for time until onset and censoring.

**Analytical Plan**:

- Define **time-to-event variable**:  
  - Event: `Met_criteria_for_dementia == 1`  
  - Time: `(diagnostic_year - form_year)` for each subject, capped at max possible (e.g., 30 years)

- Create a **survival dataset** with:
  - `time_to_dementia`
  - `censor_status` (1 = censored, 0 = event)
  - Covariates: age, sex, education, race, MMSE, CDR

- Apply **Cox proportional hazards regression**:
  $$
  h(t|X) = h_0(t)\exp(\beta_1 \cdot \text{age} + \sum \beta_j X_j)
  $$
  Interpret hazard ratio (HR) per one-year increase in age.

- **Validate proportional hazards assumption**:  
  Use Schoenfeld residuals and scaled Schoenfeld residual plots. If violated, apply time-varying covariates or stratification.

- Conduct **subgroup analyses**:
  - By race (Black/African American vs. others)
  - By gender
  - By education level

> 📌 *Why this matters*: Unlike cross-sectional logistic regression, survival analysis accounts for time-to-diagnosis and allows for better estimation of *risk accumulation* over time — essential when studying age-related progression.
