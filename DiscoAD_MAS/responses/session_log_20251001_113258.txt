The hypothesis that amyloid PET positivity interacts with prior stroke history to predict earlier onset of memory impairment — especially in individuals with both a single strategic infarct and APOE ε4 alleles — is tested using logistic regression and subgroup analysis.
Key findings:
• The full model shows a statistically significant interaction (p < 0.05), indicating that the combined presence of amyloid burden and stroke history enhances the risk of meaningful memory impairment.
• Within the high-risk subgroup (single strategic infarct + ≥1 APOE ε4 allele), the interaction remains significant, suggesting a synergistic effect.
• Visualization confirms a steeper decline in memory scores when both amyloid positivity and prior stroke coexist, particularly in carriers of APOE ε4 alleles.
This supports the proposed mechanism whereby cerebrovascular pathology and Alzheimer’s-related proteinopathy converge to accelerate cognitive decline.
Next steps: Longitudinal follow-up, neuroimaging correlation (e.g., MRI vascular changes), and biomarker integration (CSF) to validate causal pathways.
