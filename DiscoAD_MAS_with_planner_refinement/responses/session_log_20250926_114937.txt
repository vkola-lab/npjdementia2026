### Option 2: Non-parametric Mann-Whitney U Test  
- Alternative if data violate assumptions of normality or equal variance.  
- Compares median completion times across groups without assuming a specific distribution.  
- Advantages: Robust to outliers and skewed distributions common in behavioral test scores.  
- Interpretation: If significant, supports the observed trend but does not directly state "faster," only "different."

### Option 3: Stratified Analysis by Age or Gender  
- Subdivide the sample by key demographic covariates (age, gender, education level).  
- Run subgroup analyses (e.g., “Do young women on antidepressants perform better?”) to explore potential confounding factors.  
- Strategy: Identify if the effect differs by demographic — could reveal heterogeneity or bias.  
- Risk: Overfitting or reduced power in small subgroups. Use caution and report with confidence intervals.

### Option 4: Regression Modeling (Linear Model with Group as Predictive Variable)

- Output: Estimates of coefficient, p-values, and adjusted R² to assess predictive strength.  
- Advantage: Allows control for covariates (if available) such as baseline cognitive performance or comorbidities.  

### Option 5: Sensitivity Analysis with Outlier Removal
